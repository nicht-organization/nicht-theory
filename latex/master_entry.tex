\begin{abstract}
Classical empirical logic relies on positive assertion ($1$-logic), creating observation noise, assertion pressure ($P_A$), and systemic friction ($\text{Wéi}$). \textbf{Nicht-Theory} replaces assertion accumulation with apophatic subtraction ($\neg X$). We define the active present ($1$) as a transient, noisy aperture and establish systemic stability in the zero-baseline: the \textbf{Sufficient Invariant Baseline ($B_0$)}. When assertion pressure and sufficiency pressure decay ($\lim_{t \to \infty} (P_A + P_S) = 0$), system friction vanishes into 0-consistency.
\end{abstract}

\section{Core Inversion \& Axioms}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sufficient Invariant Baseline ($B_0$):} The uncreated, zero-friction potential field into which elapsed events permanently settle.
    \item \textbf{Irreal Present Aperture ($1$):} The passing, fluctuating disturbance aperture characterized by measurement friction and noise ($\sigma$).
    \item \textbf{Assertion Pressure ($P_A$):} The energy cost required to construct, assert, and defend positive empirical claims in the active state.
    \item \textbf{Apophatic Negation ($\neg X$):} The operation of subtracting speculative assertions: $\neg X \implies 0$.
\end{itemize}
