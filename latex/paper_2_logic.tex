\begin{abstract}
Classical rationalism treats paradoxes—Gödelian incompleteness, set-theoretic contradictions, semantic self-reference, and physical infinite regressions—as isolated system failures or fundamental limits of human cognition. Under \textbf{Nicht-Theory}, we show that every paradox is a single phenomenon: the point where forced positive assertion ($1$-logic, $P_A > 0, P_S > 0$) exceeds aperture capacity and generates structural friction ($\text{Wéi}$). We formally acknowledge the meta-condition of this work: using formal linguistic structures ($1$-logic) to demonstrate the primacy of the zero-baseline ($B_0$) is inherently self-referential. However, as an operational tool ($\neg X$), apophatic subtraction relieves systemic tension without adding dogmatic weight. We present a unified resolution across classical paradox classes, demonstrating that $0$-consistency provides a friction-free model for cognition, computing, and social systems.
\end{abstract}
