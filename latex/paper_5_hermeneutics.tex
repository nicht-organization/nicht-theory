\begin{abstract}
Classical linguistics and hermeneutics operate on positive assertion ($1$-logic), treating words as rigid containers of truth. This forced containment creates semantic friction ($W\text{éi}$), interpretive ambiguity, and linguistic paradoxes. Paper V applies apophatic subtraction ($\neg X$) to human language and aesthetics. We prove that symbolic communication achieves maximum clarity not by accumulating positive assertions ($P_A \to \max$), but by clearing interpretive noise until the unconditioned baseline ($B_0$) is self-evident.
\end{abstract}
