\begin{abstract}
Modern Artificial Intelligence—specifically Large Language Models (LLMs) and deep neural architectures—operates on positive token assertion ($1$-logic), forcing probability distributions to complete sequences even under incomplete context. This structural assertion pressure ($P_A > 0$) generates hallucinations, compute inefficiency, and extreme energy consumption. Paper IV introduces the \textbf{Apophatic Inference Engine (AIE)}. Rather than forcing continuous positive probability matching, the AIE evaluates incoming information against the \textbf{Sufficient Invariant Baseline ($B_0$)}. By applying apophatic subtraction ($\neg X$), the engine filters out speculative noise ($\sigma \to 0$), enabling non-assertive processing. When probability margins fall below threshold, the system does not hallucinate an answer; it drops transient degrees of freedom and relaxes into $0$-consistency—validating silence and unasserted potential as structurally optimal outputs.
\end{abstract}
